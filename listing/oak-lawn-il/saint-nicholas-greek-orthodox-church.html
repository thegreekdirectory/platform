Saint Nicholas Greek Orthodox Church

A historic Patriarchal Shrine built on sacrifice, faith, and legacy.

Call Email Website Directions
Established in May 1927 under the leadership of the Very Rev. Fr. Daniel Gambrilis, the parish of St. Nicholas Greek Orthodox Church was built on the immense sacrifices of its founding families, who went so far as to mortgage their own homes to secure the parish's future. By the 1930s, the original building was fully renovated into a traditional Byzantine house of worship, and the 1940s marked the expansion of the Homer Greek School, which continues to preserve Greek language and culture today. In the 1960s, the church was honored with a designation as a Patriarchal Shrine. To better serve its growing membership, the parish relocated from Chicago to its current site in Oak Lawn, completing a gymnasium and community center that hosted Divine Liturgies until the main church was finished. Consecrated by Archbishop Iakovos in July 1974—an event documented in the World Book Encyclopedia Yearbook—the magnificent new sanctuary officially entered a mortgage-free era by the 1980s, alongside the creation of a forward-thinking Legacy Endowment Fund under the Very Rev. Fr. Gabriel Karambis.
The late 1990s and 2000s ushered in a major era of structural renewal and spiritual beautification. Through an overwhelming building campaign and an extensive iconography project led by the Very Rev. Fr. Timothy Bakakos, the church, classrooms, and community facilities were completely restored to pristine condition. In 2001, the parish achieved national recognition by hosting the National Oratorical Festival, which included a pastoral visit from His Eminence Archbishop Demetrios of America. Today, St. Nicholas remains a vibrant and deeply collaborative spiritual home, offering an array of long-standing ministries—including the Ladies Philoptochos Society, Sunday Church School, youth athletics, and cultural dance troupes—while bringing the community together each year through its beloved annual festival and shared Orthodox faith.
